# Qev 1.0: comparison with same-size decision models

Snapshot: 12 November 2026. Qev's [OpenDecisions](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jgeuter/OpenDecisions) results use a 2,352-item development set, not a fresh independent test. Capability averages the benchmark's intelligence and calibration scores; it is not accuracy. Public-231 accuracy is a separate local diagnostic detailed below. Unless marked local, competitor scores are reported by their authors or the benchmark authors using their own serving configurations. Tables list identified comparators, not a complete ranking or proof of global state of the art. Different benchmarks cannot be subtracted.

The 82.2 and 79.3 results are reported in [Jeff 1.1's same-set evaluation table](https://huggingface.co/jgeuter/Jeff-1.1-Qwen-27B). The [OpenDecisions leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jgeuter/OpenDecisions/blob/main/README.md) reports 67.7 for Jeff 1.0, versus 68.0 on its model card. Different service configurations can affect results; differences around two points should not be treated as decisive. Our 4B target against the identified 82.2 reference is at least **78.09 Capability** (95% of 82.2); Qev 1.0 does not meet it. This is a provisional validation target, not proof of generalization. Kev's local 0.8B result gives a provisional 95%-of-reference target of **56.19 Capability**; the experimental pilot at 50.45 falls 5.73 points short using unrounded scores. The local Valen 2B result gives a provisional 95%-of-reference target of **59.02 Capability**; Qev 1.0 at 55.23 is 3.79 points short. None of these references is established as global same-size SOTA.

The 2B OpenDecisions comparison uses 2,352 identical inputs and the published scorer: Valen's local run scored 62.12 Capability (48.67 Intelligence, 75.57 Calibration) versus Qev's 55.23 Capability, an 11.10% relative gap. Valen's current local weights match its pinned Hub revision after the run; hashes were not captured at both run boundaries. The native request renderings differ, Valen's model card says its training data is not public, and source overlap and scorer parity remain unverified. This is a public development comparison, not independent generalization evidence.

On the separate [Atlan Decision Bench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/atlanai/decision-bench) 949 text-only cases, Qev 1.0 4B's local diagnostic scored **749/949 (78.93%)**, versus [Kev 4B's published run](https://github.com/atlanai/decision-bench/tree/412d13b19adaf75e09c017afabb893c4828fe080/results/bench-v4/kev-4b) **795/949 (83.77%)**: a 4.85 percentage-point, 5.79% relative deficit. Qev answered 912 and refused 37 overlength cases; Kev answered all 949. On those 912 shared answered cases, Qev/Kev hard-gold Brier was 0.269/0.229 and NLL 0.488/0.431. Qev used its unchanged checkpoint, 2,048-token default and native local candidate rendering; Kev used a hosted OpenRouter checkpoint. Both received the benchmark's judgment-policy wording, but prompts, runtimes and potentially precision differ. State leakage, source overlap and full scorer parity remain unverified; this public Choice-only diagnostic is not an independent generalization result or an official Qev leaderboard run.

Download counts are Hugging Face's rolling monthly counts observed 12 November 2026 for the linked original repositories, not deduplicated people, quality measurements or sums across quantizations. Kev and Decider are included because of their adoption and relevance; popularity itself does not establish they are the strongest on this benchmark.

We are pursuing fresh, leakage-screened paired evaluations and improved training at all three sizes. Existing Qev training source provenance, rights and independent generalization remain unresolved; see the individual model cards. We will update these tables when comparable, reproducible evidence is available.

The evaluation plan targets at least three typed-decision benchmarks: OpenDecisions, [JevBench's public 231-item subset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/kshitijthakkar/nirnaya-jevbench-public-eval-v1), and [typed-decisions](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LocalLLaMA/typed-decisions) (2,000 test questions). The public-231 diagnostic results above are accuracy, not OpenDecisions Capability; all three Qev typed-decisions results are preliminary local diagnostics. Typed-decisions uses teacher-derived targets; some competing models train on its training split, so distinguish specialists from generalists. None of the OpenDecisions gaps can be transferred to another benchmark. The public 231 subset is not the full sealed JevBench leaderboard.

For the same 2,000 typed-decisions questions, Kev 0.8B scored 883/2,000 (44.15%) and Qev 1.0 0.8B scored 870/2,000 (43.50%); all were answered. Kev/Qev Brier against teacher distributions was 0.205/0.257 and KL 0.362/0.455 (lower is better). A 2,000-draw paired bootstrap by 400 source scenarios gives Qev minus Kev accuracy an exploratory 95% interval of −3.45 to +2.20 percentage points. Kev used its native float32 predictor and Qev its native scorer, with different prompt renderings; the Windows Kev runtime uses a shim. Teacher quality, scorer parity, training overlap, and independent generalization remain unverified. This comparison does not establish a statistically decisive accuracy winner.

On those same typed-decisions cases, Kev 4B scored 1,314/2,000 (65.70%) and Qev 1.0 4B scored 1,290/2,000 (64.50%); both answered all cases. Kev/Qev Brier against teacher distributions was 0.124/0.137 and KL 0.219/0.245. By type, Qev/Kev were 387/387 correct on 600 Choice, 407/432 on 600 Noul and 496/495 on 800 Score. The paired 2,000-draw, 400-scenario bootstrap interval for Qev minus Kev accuracy is −3.05 to +0.70 percentage points. Kev used its native bf16 predictor on Windows, versus Qev's native local scorer; prompts and precision differ, and teacher labels, scorer parity and source overlap remain unverified. These public-development results do not establish independent generalization or a definitive winner.

Valen 1.5 2B scored 1,383/2,000 (69.15%) versus Qev 1.0 2B's 1,122/2,000 (56.10%), both answering all cases. Valen/Qev Brier against teacher distributions was 0.112/0.208 and KL 0.222/0.388. By type, Qev/Valen were 339/426 correct on 600 Choice, 381/456 on 600 Noul and 402/501 on 800 Score. The paired 2,000-draw, 400-scenario bootstrap interval for Qev minus Valen accuracy is −15.75 to −10.55 percentage points. The native model implementations and prompts differ, local Valen weights were not independently hashed, and teacher-label quality, scorer parity and source overlap remain unverified. This public development comparison is not independent generalization evidence.

Public-231 figures are preliminary local diagnostics on the same 231 published items, matched by ID to the pinned upstream source; accuracy is correct / 231, with unsupported Qev cases counted wrong. Qev 0.8B/2B/4B each refused 36 overlength inputs at their 2,048-token limit and answered 195; their answered-only accuracies were 66.67% / 75.38% / 81.03%. Valen 2B answered all 231. At 95% of Valen's 81.39%, the provisional 2B target on this suite is 77.32%; Qev 1.0 is 13.68 points below that target. We used each model's native decision interface and different prompt renderings; the Qev and Valen score adapters have not been independently certified against the upstream benchmark scorer, and training/source overlap has not been fully audited. The 17.75-point gap is a provisional local observation, not a leaderboard rank or uncontaminated estimate.

Kev 0.8B answered 231/231 and scored 146/231 (63.20%) in a local run of its native predictor at Hub revision `bf75a6a8848ea6960ff2ed108d9ed44c2941174f` and upstream code revision `2074cc44256200bb37540103fa4572901faa10f3`. It is 16 cases, or 6.93 percentage points, above Qev 1.0 0.8B's 130/231 (56.28%); values rounded separately can differ by 0.01 point. Kev used an 8,192-token state limit and Qev 2,048, so Qev's 36 refusals affect the gap. Kev's predictor ran on Windows with a compatibility shim and package versions outside its declared runtime; prompt rendering, scorer parity and training overlap need further verification. The unreleased pilot at an experimental 4,096-token limit scored 142/231 (61.47%), four cases behind Kev, within 5% of this single observed reference score only; it does not establish the multi-benchmark SOTA goal. The pilot was trained at a shorter context and one previously answered prediction flipped between runs.

Mapika Decider 2B answered 231/231 and scored 179/231 (77.49%) in a local text-only run at Hub revision `7614a6c7ea5a047aa7d7bcfb1a0015469381c3e3`, upstream code revision `91b6fa9b94ab6c4dc2c63d3b14d413243b4fffcc`, bf16 and an 8,192-token state limit. It is 32 cases (13.85 percentage points) above Qev 1.0 2B's 147/231. We used the model card's separately fitted Choice/Noul/Score temperatures 2.465/3.185/2.833. Its shipped Noul default is 0.05, which the author says improves a benchmark-specific rule at the cost of calibration; this result uses the fitted setting. Its native API rounds probabilities to four decimals before our normalization. The author reports using benchmark readouts in checkpoint selection; model rendering, context, overlap and scorer parity differ or remain unverified. These are preliminary local diagnostics, not an independent ranking.

Broader 2B diagnostic on public-231: Qev 1.0 answered 195/231 (84.42% coverage), while Valen and Decider each answered all 231. On the same 195 cases that all answered, hard-gold multiclass Brier was 0.330 for Qev, 0.205 for Valen and 0.247 for Decider (lower is better); NLL was 0.572, 0.349 and 0.442 respectively. These probability metrics exclude Qev's 36 refusals and do not establish that the gold labels represent true uncertainty. An exploratory 2,000-replicate cluster bootstrap of all-item accuracy gives a 95% interval for Qev minus Valen of −24.0 to −11.6 percentage points, and Qev minus Decider of −19.9 to −7.5; it reflects this public development sample, not an independent final test. Different native interfaces and context limits remain material. Comparable latency, memory, independent OOD robustness and semantic-overlap audits are not yet available; no all-around winner is established by these figures.

Kev 4B scored 172/231 (74.46%) versus Qev 1.0 4B's 158/231 (68.40%); Kev answered all items and Qev refused 36 overlength cases. On the 195 cases both answered, hard-gold Brier was 0.261 for Qev and 0.262 for Kev, and NLL was 0.468 and 0.457 respectively (lower is better). The exploratory 2,000-replicate cluster-bootstrap 95% interval for Qev minus Kev all-item accuracy is −12.2 to 0.0 percentage points. Kev's pinned revision `6cfce5c2fa4b4bd64026336ab649c5ca78857d52` ran through its native predictor in bf16 on Windows, with an 8,192-token state limit; its published evaluation uses fp32. Qev uses a 2,048-token limit and a different rendering. Scorer parity, source overlap and independent generalization are unverified. On a separate small synthetic maximum-selection diagnostic, Kev scored 96/96 and Qev 70/96; neither changed answers on the 24 option-reorder or 24 irrelevant-text pairs. This single artificial task is not a general ranking and related task families may occur in training.

An inference-only Qev 4B experiment raised the limit to 4,096 tokens without truncation or changing its weights or temperature. It answered all 231 cases and scored 174/231 (75.32%); Kev scored 172/231. On all 231, Qev/Kev hard-gold Brier was 0.307/0.323, NLL 0.557/0.569, and 10-bin top-label ECE 0.074/0.041, respectively (lower is better). The exploratory paired cluster-bootstrap 95% interval for Qev minus Kev accuracy was −4.5 to +6.2 percentage points. One previously answered Qev prediction changed between runs. The longer inference setting exceeds Qev's training limit, is not its released default, and on the long-input run total GPU usage reached about 15.7 GiB on the local 16 GiB card. The public cases are development diagnostics with different native renderings, unknown overlap and unverified scorer parity; the small accuracy difference does not establish an overall winner or long-context generalization.

Exploratory batch-1 timings on the same 24 short synthetic inputs on an RTX 5070 Ti after three warmups: Qev 4B median/p95 93.71/100.46 ms and peak allocated tensor memory 7.97 GiB; Kev 4B 82.51/98.79 ms and 8.04 GiB. Timings used native implementations with different prompt rendering and were measured in separate runs; Qev's run coincided with a background download. Both are small, unoptimized local measurements, not production latency or definitive speed rankings.

On OpenDecisions, the same pinned Kev native predictor answered all 2,352 items and scored 59.14 Capability (31.18 intelligence, 87.10 calibration), compared with Qev 1.0 0.8B at 46.85 and the unreleased pilot at 50.45. These are development diagnostics, not independently held-out results; the same runtime, rendering, scorer-parity and overlap limitations apply. Capability is not accuracy. The official local scorer was applied to the recorded keyed probability vectors.

An earlier unreleased 0.8B checkpoint trained on a broad Tasksource-derived mixture scored 49.32 OpenDecisions Capability and 133/231 (57.58%) on public-231; it refused the same 36 long inputs as Qev 1.0. On the 195 cases both answered, its hard-gold Brier/NLL were 0.458/0.821 versus Qev 1.0's 0.418/0.703 (lower is better). On a small generated invariance diagnostic it scored 41/96 versus Qev 1.0's 63/96; its prior training included related procedural tasks. These are diagnostic tradeoffs, not an independent generalization result. Source rights and semantic overlap remain unresolved, and this checkpoint is not promoted.

The local 0.8B pilot is a separate one-pass LoRA and head trained on 1,000 teacher-target decisions from the typed-decisions training workflows, with temperature fitted on separate scenarios. Its held-out typed test score is 1,163/2,000, with Brier 0.1377 and KL 0.2366. It scored 50.45 Capability on all 2,352 OpenDecisions development items and 130/231 on public-231; the same 36 overlength inputs were counted incorrect. Exact normalized scenario matches across its training and these evaluations were absent, but semantic overlap, scorer equivalence, rights and independent generalization remain unverified. It is not a released model or a clean independent benchmark result; the specialist's reported 75.1% uses a different setup.

The separate local 2B one-pass pilot used the same 1,000 teacher-target workflow decisions and separate calibration scenarios. It scored 51.93 OpenDecisions Capability versus Qev 1.0 2B's 55.23, and 1,388/2,000 (69.40%) on held-out typed-workflow scenarios versus 56.10% for Qev 1.0. Typed Brier/KL against teacher targets were 0.0925/0.1739. On public-231 it answered all cases at an experimental 4,096-token inference limit and scored 162/231 (70.13%), versus Qev 1.0's 147/231 (63.64%) and Valen's 188/231 (81.39%). This is a mixed outcome with an OpenDecisions regression: the workflow test is related to training, the longer context exceeds the training limit, rendering differs, overlap and scorer parity are unverified, and no independent generalization or release claim follows. Future training prioritizes broad task generalization rather than tuning to these evaluation cases.
